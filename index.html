KopaAlert

kopa · to buy on credit
LIVE · kopaalert.shop

Kenyan shops sell on credit and track it in exercise books. Customers forget, or avoid paying. KopaAlert keeps the record and sends polite SMS reminders automatically, so owners get paid without the awkward calls.

What I built

  • Automatic SMS reminders before, on and after the due date, as plain SMS that reach feature phones too (Africa's Talking)
  • Loans, payments, top-ups and overpayment credit, with a full ledger and M-Pesa-style PDF statements
  • Customer credit ratings and automatic blacklisting of long-overdue customers
  • Import a whole notebook at once from Excel/CSV, or type it in
  • Multi-branch businesses: each branch has its own customers, a branch switcher for owners, and staff who only see their branch
  • Staff controls: deactivate/reactivate, opening hours per branch with a "today only" override, and an activity log of who changed what
  • Subscription billing, SMS allowances, referrals ("bring a business, get a month free") and a super-admin console
  • Installable app (PWA) with an offline page, for owners on budget phones

The hard part

Keeping every business's data private, and every employee inside their branch and their working hours, in the database itself, using PostgreSQL row-level security and triggers, so it holds even if someone bypasses the app, and even mid-session. Every change was rehearsed on a copy of the live schema before touching real businesses.

LindaMeru

linda · to protect
FINAL-YEAR PROJECT · BUILT FOR IGEMBE NORTH

Livestock rustling and insecurity hit Meru's border areas hardest, where the network is weakest and phones are oldest. LindaMeru gives officers a way to report incidents with no signal, and commanders a live picture of where to send help.

What I built

  • An offline-first app for officers: reports are kept safely on the phone and sync once, by themselves, when the network returns
  • A crime map with sub-county boundaries and statistical hot spots (Getis-Ord Gi*), plus migration routes, grazing areas and threat zones
  • An ERP for stations, vehicles, officers, shift rosters, fuel and drones, with readiness at a glance
  • Dispatch advice that ranks vehicles by travel time along Meru's real roads (OpenStreetMap + PostGIS), not straight-line distance
  • A commander dashboard, community tip inbox, and an audit trail where each record is sealed to the one before it, so history can't be quietly changed

The hard part

Designing for no network and very old phones: no framework and no build step on the frontend, a queue in IndexedDB that never sends a report twice, and road-network routing that falls back gracefully where roads aren't mapped. All of it covered by 210 automated tests against a real PostGIS database.

SomaOrbit

soma · to study
V1 BUILT · IN SYSTEM QA

CBC schools juggle paper registers, spreadsheets and separate tools for fees, exams and parents. SomaOrbit puts a whole school in one system, and serves many schools from one deployment without any school ever seeing another's data.

What I built

  • 57 modules across 165 pages for six roles: super admin, school admin, teacher, staff, parent and student
  • CBC competencies, assessments, exams and report cards; timetables and attendance (works offline)
  • Fees, payments with an automated webhook, fee structures, discounts and a finance ledger
  • School departments: QR gate passes and visitor log, health and sick bay, transport, kitchen, library, labs and co-curricular
  • SMS and email to guardians

The hard part

Multi-tenant security for children's data: row-level security across 99 database migrations, extra verification (MFA) on 12 sensitive areas, two-person approval for refunds and fee waivers, audit trails, and a super admin who deliberately can't see inside schools.

How I work

Real users deserve careful engineering.

The habits behind all three projects. Ask me about any of them in an interview; I can show the receipts.

01

Security lives in the database

Who can see what is enforced by PostgreSQL row-level security, not just hidden buttons. I test it by switching between roles on a copy of the database.

02

Built for real Kenyan conditions

Offline-first apps, budget phones, plain SMS that reaches feature phones, Kenya time and Kiswahili. If it only works on fast Wi-Fi, it doesn't work.

03

Careful with live systems

Read-only investigation first, database changes rehearsed on a copy of the live schema, and the database deployed before the code that depends on it.

04

Honest records

Every notable change gets a dated changelog entry that says what was verified and what wasn't. No "done" that isn't done.
